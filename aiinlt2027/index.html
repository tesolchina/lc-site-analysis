What is being asked

EDB is inviting quotations from a Service Provider to organise the third edition of the AIinLT Summit as a flagship of its Digital Education Week 2027. The provider must run a 2-day Summit (≥3 keynotes, 60 paper presentations, 1–2 expert panels) and a 2-day Workshop Series (≥20 workshops), secure clustered venues for 500+/100+/70+ audiences, deploy a minimum on-site team, manage publicity, registration, catering, AI-assisted simultaneous interpreting and transcription, video capture, an evaluation, and a public resource package — with a dissemination tail running to December 2027.

The make-or-break: the experience gate

Essential requirement (disqualifying if not met). The Service Provider must show a proven track record of organising international conferences related to (applied) linguistics or AI in education, with total attendance exceeding 4,000 within the past 10 years, and those conferences must have included keynote speeches, parallel sessions, expert panel discussions, and pre-/post-conference workshops. The proposed Event Management Team Leader must separately show a multi-day international conference with >4,000 attendance in the past 5 years, requiring stage/venue set-up, guest reception, catering and manpower deployment.

This gate — not price and not the plans — is the first thing to resolve. If the Language Centre cannot itself point to qualifying events, the only viable route is to partner (sub-contracting is permitted, with undertakings) or to bid under a wider institutional/HKBU record with a professional conference organiser as team leader. See §6 for our record and the gap.

Prior editions — an incumbent is already ahead

AIinLT 2025 (4–7 Jul 2025) and AIinLT 2026 (21–24 Jun 2026) were both hosted by PolyU (Department of English and Communication) with EDB and SCOLAR. The 2026 edition drew over 5,000 attendances, with 93 parallel sessions, 22 workshops, and six keynotes. PolyU therefore satisfies the experience gate outright and can be expected to bid again. Sources in §5 and the appendix.

2What EDB is asking for — scope, deliverables, payment▶

2.1 Theme & sub-themes

Theme: “AI in Language Education: Elevating Practice, Enduring Sustainability, and Leading a Paradigm Shift” — positioned as a progression from awareness (2025) → theory-to-practice (2026) → deepening implementation and sustainability (2027).

Eleven sub-themes (all must be comprehensively covered by the 20 workshop topics): (i) Sustaining & Deepening AI Integration; (ii) Redefining the Educator: AI as Collaborator, Teacher as Architect; (iii) AI-Augmented Personalisation & Learner Autonomy; (iv) Next-Gen Assessment: Continuous, Formative, Proficiency-Based; (v) AI Literacy for All; (vi) Inclusive & Equitable AI; (vii) Human-AI Synergy in the Classroom; (viii) AI for Smarter Language Education Administration; (ix) Multimodal & Immersive Language Learning; (x) From AI User to AI Creator; (xi) Evolving Policies & Future Directions.

  • Establish a Summit Committee led by at least one faculty member, with Government Representatives as members.
  • Venues: clustered, within walking distance; main venue for opening/closing for 500+; ≥5 parallel-session venues for 100+ each; ≥5 workshop venues for 70+ each; plus press-conference and media-interview venues. Accessible by public transport or free shuttle (provider’s responsibility).
  • Manpower on event days (minimum): 5 academic staff, 5 technical support, 5 administrative support, 40 student helpers; plus a training session for all student helpers run by the provider and SCOLAR.

  • Collaboration across faculties/institutions encouraged; other tasks as specified by Government Representatives.

“Similar event” for the provider = an international conference in (applied) linguistics or AI in education with total attendance >4,000, including keynotes, parallel sessions, panels and pre-/post-conference workshops. For the team leader = a multi-day international conference with >4,000 attendance requiring stage/venue set-up, guest reception, catering and manpower deployment. Experience must be claimed in Schedules B/D with documentary proof, or it scores zero.

3.3 Final score

Weighted technical = 70 × (your technical mark ÷ the highest technical mark among conforming quotations).
Weighted price = 30 × (lowest conforming price ÷ your price).
Total = weighted technical + weighted price.

Consequence: the highest technical bidder automatically takes the full 70, and everyone else is scaled down against it. Price is a 30-point lever — a bid at twice the lowest price would score 15/30. Innovation/ESG proposals (20 of the 85 Part-A marks) are effectively “free” marks for good ideas and should be treated as a must, not an extra.

4.1 What the benchmark tells us

  • Incumbent: PolyU has hosted both editions and comfortably clears the >4,000 attendance gate (2026: >5,000). Expect them to score 10/10 and 5/5 on experience.
  • Scale is real: the 2027 requirement (60 papers, 20 workshops, 4,000) is below what 2026 actually delivered (93 sessions, 22 workshops, >5,000) — so the required scale is achievable by an experienced organiser, but not trivially.
  • What made it work: free entry; strong government backing (Secretary for Education opening); a school-teacher focus; hands-on workshops rather than talks only; cross-sector panels mixing school educators with academics; and a heavy digital capture + reference-materials tail (a public thematic website).
  • The 2027 twist: the new emphasis on sustainability and implementation depth, plus the explicit AI-assisted interpreting/transcription and a public resource package, raises the technology and post-production bar.

6.2 The gap (honest assessment)

Based on the record above, the Language Centre does not obviously hold a qualifying “similar event”: a multi-day international conference with >4,000 attendance, involving stage/venue set-up, guest reception, catering and large-scale manpower deployment — the essential requirement for both the Service Provider and the Event Management Team Leader.

Three ways this could still be bridged, all of which need checking before committing:

  1. Wider HKBU record. The quote can be submitted by HKBU (not just the LC). HKBU as a whole hosts large international conferences/anniversaries — if any qualifying event (>4,000; multi-day; with workshops) exists in the last 10 years, it may satisfy criterion (6). Needs verification with the Research Office / Events.
  2. Partner (sub-contracting allowed). A professional conference organiser or another faculty with a qualifying event could serve as Service Provider / Team Leader, with the LC supplying academic content — declared in the Information Schedule with a sub-contractor’s undertaking (Appendix D).
  3. Consortium. Team up with an institution that has run a comparable summit, or support rather than lead (e.g., partner with the incumbent), preserving LC involvement without carrying delivery risk.

If none of these hold, the quotation will fail Stage 2 regardless of how good the plans are — so this is the first question, not the last.

10Questions for discussion▶

Grouped for the 14 Sep feedback to Joshua / Hok Chung. The starred ones block everything else.

A. Go / no-go & eligibility blocking
Without a qualifying event, no plan matters.
  1. ★ Do we (LC) — or HKBU as an institution — hold a qualifying international conference in the last 10 years: multi-day, >4,000 attendance, in (applied) linguistics or AI in education, with keynotes + parallel sessions + panels + pre/post workshops?
  2. ★ Who could credibly be named as the Event Management Team Leader (multi-day >4,000 event with stage/venue/catering/manpower, past 5 years)? Is there anyone in HKBU Events/Research Office?
  3. If neither exists, is the Centre willing to partner (sub-contract) with a PCO / another faculty — or should we decline / support only?
  4. Is there institutional (senior management / VPTL) appetite for HKBU to be the named Service Provider at all?
B. Competitive position
PolyU hosted 2025 and 2026 and will likely bid.
  1. Are we willing to compete against the incumbent, or is a partnership/support role more realistic and valuable?
  2. If we bid, who leads the Design Plan (30 marks) and the Implementation Plan (25 marks) — and what is our genuine differentiator (e.g., HKBU’s AI-in-teaching tools, GenAI CoP, research base)?
  3. Can we assemble 3 credible innovation proposals (+15) and ESG proposals (+5) to capture the full 20 bonus marks?
C. Venue & logistics
The venue spec is unusually exacting.
  1. Does HKBU have, in one walkable cluster: a 500+ hall, ≥5 rooms for 100+, ≥5 rooms for 70+, and press/media rooms — for four days in late June 2027?
  2. If not, do we go external (HKCEC/hotel) with shuttle service? Who bears that cost, and does it fit the price score?
  3. June 2027 clashes: HKBU academic calendar, room availability, and other LC events — what is booked?
D. People & capacity
~2–3 FTE-years plus 55+ on-site staff.
  1. Who would be released from normal duties, and can the Centre fund backfill/secondments?
  2. How do we recruit and train 40 student helpers (with the mandatory joint training with SCOLAR)?
  3. Do we buy in a PCO for delivery, or run it in-house? Either way, what is the minimum viable team?
E. Money
No ceiling is published; price is 30% of the score.
  1. What is our value range — is the indicative HK$3.6M–8.1M realistic, and what would HKBU’s finance/accounting require for a fixed-price bid?
  2. How do we price against the 30 × (lowest ÷ ours) formula without losing money?
  3. Who underwrites the risk of attendance >4,000 and a 12-month tail to Dec 2027 (cash-flow 20/50/30)?
F. Content & compliance
All 11 sub-themes must be covered.
  1. Who maps the 20 workshop topics to the 11 sub-themes, with a Chinese/English and international/Mainland/local balance?
  2. Do we have (or can we recruit) the keynote and presenter network for June 2027?
  3. Data protection (PDPO/GDPR), recording consent, accessibility, and IP for videos/resource package — who owns that compliance work?
G. Immediate logistics
The clock is already running.
  1. The briefing registration deadline (9 Sep) has passed — can we still register for the 14 Sep 10:30 briefing (Appendix B)? If not, how do we get questions answered (written enquiries to eani@edb.gov.hk)?
  2. What is the expected content and stance of our 14 Sep feedback to Joshua / Hok Chung — “not feasible”, “explore with partner”, or “prepare a bid”?
  3. If we proceed, who is the single accountable owner and what is the milestone plan to 29 Oct?
